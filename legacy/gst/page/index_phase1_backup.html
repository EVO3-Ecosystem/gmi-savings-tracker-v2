Where savings can come from

Operating budget by what an agency can change. Most of the budget is staff, grants, client payments such as Medicaid, and debt service. Savings work targets controllable purchases: contracts, supplies, equipment, vehicles, energy, telecom and leases. Solid bars are actual spending; faded bars are budgets.

Savings tracker

Arrives in phase 2: each GMI workstream's reported savings, labeled recurring, one-time or unconfirmed, beside the change in the state's own payments.

Opportunities

Arrives in phases 3–4: eight detectors scan every spending code at every agency and rank where the next $50M is likely to be.

Calculator

Arrives in phase 4: set savings levers and assumptions, see the range of outcomes and the chance of reaching $50M, and save scenarios for yourself.

Trends

Vendors

Controllable purchases per employee

Each agency's controllable purchases divided by its budgeted full-time positions, for the selected year: actual spending where the year has actuals, otherwise that year's budget. This is the base for peer benchmarking: phase 3 groups similar agencies so comparisons are fair. Agencies with fewer than 50 positions are left out, since one contract swings their figure.

Method

Controllable purchases are operating spending an agency can renegotiate or reduce: contractual services, supplies, equipment, vehicles, fuel and utilities, communications, travel, rent and insurance. Left out: salaries, contractual payroll, grants, capital, client and provider payments (Medicaid, purchase of care, other fee-for-service, and pass-through programs such as the Benefit Exchange's reinsurance, provider reimbursements, foster care maintenance and energy assistance), debt service, and charges between state agencies (DoIT and in-state allocations, rent paid to DGS).

Budget stages. The budget file holds one stage per year: actuals for FY2017–FY2020 and FY2023–FY2025, a working budget for FY2021 and FY2026, the appropriation for FY2022 and the Legislative Appropriation for FY2027. The file still labels FY2027 "Governors Allowance"; DBM's Operating Budget site calls it the Legislative Appropriation and its figures match to the dollar.

Master lists. Payments carry an agency code from FY2020; earlier names are mapped by that code, then by hand for renamed agencies (for example DHMH to the Maryland Department of Health), and capital-budget accounts are grouped as capital. MDOT's modal administrations roll up to the Department of Transportation. Payee names are grouped when they differ only by suffix or spacing, and known company families (Verizon, AT&T, utilities, IT resellers) are merged.

Built by Sierra Napier with Python. The pipeline runs monthly; it can also be deployed on AWS or Google Cloud.
