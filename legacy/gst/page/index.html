    Leads by detector

    Four automated checks run on every controllable spending code at every agency (FY2025 actual budget) and on FY2026 payments to vendors. Bars show the likely value of each detector's leads; the thin bar shows the high end. Detectors overlap, so the bars are not added. Click a bar for that detector's page.

    DetectorAgencySpending code or vendorFocus areaSpendingLikely valueRangeWhy

    Peer gap: spending per budgeted position above three in four agencies; 5–15% of the excess is counted because agencies differ in mission. Growth above trend: spending that grew 25+ points faster than the state's from FY2022 to FY2025; 10–30% of the extra growth is counted.       Method

      Controllable purchases are operating spending an agency can renegotiate or reduce: contractual services, supplies, equipment, vehicles, fuel and utilities, communications, travel, rent and insurance. Left out: salaries, contractual payroll, grants, capital, client and provider payments (Medicaid, purchase of care, other fee-for-service, and pass-through programs such as the Benefit Exchange's reinsurance, provider reimbursements, foster care maintenance and energy assistance), debt service, and charges between state agencies (DoIT and in-state allocations, rent paid to DGS).

      Focus areas are GMI's six spending areas, defined two ways. By spending code: Comptroller sub-objects for motor vehicle operation; data processing, software and computer services; communications other than postage; postage and printing; fuel and utilities; and non-DGS rent, within controllable purchases. By payee name: payments to vendors whose names identify the area, with pass-through payments removed. Spending codes are the firmer measure; payee names show which vendors.

      Spending type. FY2017–FY2025 are actual spending, FY2026 is the working budget and FY2027 is the Legislative Appropriation, as DBM's Operating Budget site states; the pipeline reads that statement on every run. Actual spending and budgets are kept apart by default, as finance reporting practice recommends, so totals never add a plan to what was spent unless you choose "Actual and budgets". Payments to vendors are always actual spending, so FY2026 payments count as actual even though the FY2026 budget is still a working budget. The main views start at FY2017; each payments detail page shows the full history from FY2008.

      Filters. Every filter starts at All. Years, focus areas and agencies take several values at once; the cards add up the selection. Clicking a bar for an area or agency filters the page by it; clicking a year adds or removes that year, and time charts keep every year on screen with the selection highlighted. A check beside the filters confirms that the parts on screen add up to the totals on screen.

      Master lists. Payments carry an agency code from FY2020; earlier names are mapped by that code, then by hand for renamed agencies, and capital-budget accounts are grouped as capital. MDOT's modal administrations roll up to the Department of Transportation. Payee names are grouped when they differ only by suffix or spacing, and known company families are merged.

      Built by Sierra Napier with Python. The pipeline runs monthly, checks itself and the dashboard before publishing, and can also be deployed on AWS or Google Cloud.
