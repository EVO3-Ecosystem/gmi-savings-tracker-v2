Method

Controllable purchases are operating spending an agency can renegotiate or reduce: contractual services, supplies, equipment, vehicles, fuel and utilities, communications, travel, rent and insurance. Left out: salaries, contractual payroll, grants, capital, client and provider payments (Medicaid, purchase of care, other fee-for-service, and pass-through programs such as the Benefit Exchange's reinsurance, provider reimbursements, foster care maintenance and energy assistance), debt service, and charges between state agencies (DoIT and in-state allocations, rent paid to DGS).

Focus areas are GMI's six spending areas, defined two ways. By spending code: Comptroller sub-objects for motor vehicle operation; data processing, software and computer services; communications other than postage; postage and printing; fuel and utilities; and non-DGS rent, within controllable purchases. By payee name: payments to vendors whose names identify the area (fuel and fleet companies, IT resellers, carriers, utilities, landlords), with pass-through payments removed. Spending codes are the firmer measure; payee names show which vendors.

Budget stages. FY2017–FY2025 are actual spending, FY2026 is the working budget and FY2027 is the Legislative Appropriation, as DBM's Operating Budget site states. The pipeline reads that statement on every run. It does not use the budget file's own stage column, which keeps the label each year had when it was loaded ("Working" for FY2021, "Appropriation" for FY2022, "Governors Allowance" for FY2027). FY2021 and FY2022 were checked program by program against the actuals DBM published in the FY2023 and FY2024 budget books: most programs match to the dollar, and the rest differ by funds paid between agencies, which the file leaves out.

Master lists. Payments carry an agency code from FY2020; earlier names are mapped by that code, then by hand for renamed agencies (for example DHMH to the Maryland Department of Health), and capital-budget accounts are grouped as capital. MDOT's modal administrations roll up to the Department of Transportation. Payee names are grouped when they differ only by suffix or spacing, and known company families (Verizon, AT&T, utilities, IT resellers) are merged.

Built by Sierra Napier with Python. The pipeline runs monthly; it can also be deployed on AWS or Google Cloud.
